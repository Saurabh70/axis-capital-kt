Start here if you know nothing about Axis Capital.

What is Axis Capital?

Axis Capital Limited (ACL) is the investment banking and institutional equities arm of Axis Bank and is a wholly owned subsidiary of Axis Bank. Source: Axis Capital

At a high level, Axis Capital sits between:

Companies / Promoters / Government / PE Funds (need capital or strategic advice) │ ▼ AXIS CAPITAL │ ▼ Institutional Investors (provide capital, invest in securities)

Its business broadly covers:

Investment Banking

Equity Capital Markets (ECM) · Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A) · Private Fund Raising · InvITs / REITs / Private Credit Syndication

Institutional Equities

Equity Research · Cash Equities · Derivatives · Sales & Trading · Corporate Access

Axis Capital itself describes its offering as a combination of ECM, M&A, private fundraising, InvIT/REIT/private-credit syndication, research, cash equities and derivatives. Source: Axis Capital

The simplest way to remember it

Axis Bank = Banking.   Axis Capital = Capital Markets + Investment Banking + Institutional Equities.

This is one of the first things you should understand — and one of the easiest to get asked directly.

Part 1 · Company

The History — ENAM → Axis Capital

Important for understanding why Axis Capital has a strong reputation in Indian capital markets — this is not a generic “long history” answer.

1984 — ENAM begins

ENAM Securities founded

ENAM started building a franchise in India’s capital markets, developing relationships with some of India’s largest companies and becoming particularly known for capital-market transactions.

1990s — Indian capital markets

SEBI registration & marquee IPOs

By 1992, ENAM had registered with SEBI and was involved in taking companies such as Infosys and Zee Entertainment public. By 1997, it was leading marquee IPOs including Marico and Essar Oil. ENAM also developed an institutional trading desk around this period. Source: Axis Capital

2000s — Technology + Investment Banking

India’s first tech IPO wave

ENAM participated in India’s first major wave of technology IPOs, including companies such as KPIT Systems and Polaris. It subsequently expanded into a fuller investment-banking platform, including M&A.

2012 — Axis Bank acquisition

The key event

Axis Bank acquired ENAM’s Investment Banking and Institutional Equities business in 2012. The business was subsequently renamed Axis Capital. Source: Axis Capital

Post-2012 — Continued expansion

Franchise built out across new areas

Examples include the RBL Bank IPO, the D-Mart IPO, L&T’s acquisition of Mindtree, InvIT/REIT transactions, new-age technology companies, renewable-energy companies and industrial businesses. Axis Capital says it led L&T’s acquisition of Mindtree in 2019, which it describes as India’s largest technology M&A transaction at the time. Source: Axis Capital

Why does the ENAM legacy matter?

A candidate should not simply say “Axis Capital has a long history.” The more important point:

Must know

ENAM gave Axis Capital capital-market relationships + transaction experience + institutional-equities capabilities — particularly relevant to ECM. Axis Capital describes itself as having maintained a leadership position in Indian ECM for more than a decade. Source: Axis Capital

So when you see Axis Capital repeatedly involved in large IPOs, this is not an accidental development — it is part of the firm’s historical DNA.

Part 1 · Company

Investment Banking

Helping companies and financial sponsors with capital raising and strategic transactions. Axis Capital’s IB franchise covers ECM, M&A, Private Fund Raising, and InvITs/REITs/Private Credit Syndication. Source: Axis Capital

Equity Capital Markets (ECM)

ECM helps companies raise money through equity-linked / public-market transactions. Major products: IPOs, QIPs, OFS, Rights Issues, Follow-on offerings, Block deals, Secondary placements.

Must know

Axis Capital says it has been a leading ECM banker for over a decade and closed 39 primary and secondary transactions in FY24. Source: Axis Capital

IPO — Initial Public Offering

Definition: A private company becomes publicly listed and offers shares to public-market investors.

Example: Company needs ₹3,000 crore → decides to IPO → investment banks advise on valuation, structure, marketing and execution → investors subscribe → company gets listed.

What does the investment bank actually do? Understand the business · analyse financials · determine transaction structure · valuation · prepare offer documents · coordinate with regulators · prepare investor presentations · market the transaction · meet institutional investors · book building · pricing · allocation · listing.

Watch out

An IPO is much more than “selling shares.” If your answer stops there, you haven’t shown you understand the role.

QIP — Qualified Institutional Placement

Definition: A listed company raises capital by issuing shares to qualified institutional buyers — typically mutual funds, insurance companies, foreign institutional investors and alternative investment funds.

Why would a company choose a QIP? It can be a relatively efficient way to raise substantial capital without conducting another IPO.

Example: Listed company wants ₹2,000 crore for expansion / debt repayment → conducts QIP → institutional investors subscribe → company receives capital.

OFS — Offer for Sale

Definition: Existing shareholders sell their shares. The company itself generally does not receive the proceeds.

Example: Government owns 20% of a PSU → wants to reduce its stake → sells shares through OFS.

M&A — Mergers & Acquisitions

Axis Capital also advises companies on strategic transactions.

Acquisition

Company A wants to acquire Company B. Axis Capital may help with valuation, deal structure, negotiation, due-diligence coordination, financing, regulatory considerations and transaction execution.

Divestiture

A company wants to sell one of its businesses. The bank can identify buyers, value the business, run the sale process, negotiate and execute.

Axis Capital highlights experience across mergers, demergers, group reorganisations and buy-side/sell-side transactions. Source: Axis Capital

Private Fund Raising

Not every company wants to go public. A company can instead raise capital from PE funds, VC funds, sovereign funds, family offices or strategic investors — Axis Capital helps match companies with suitable investors.

InvITs & REITs

InvIT — Infrastructure Investment Trust

Pool of infrastructure assets (roads, transmission, power infrastructure) → trust structure → investors buy units → receive distributions from asset cash flows.

REIT — Real Estate Investment Trust

Income-generating real estate (office buildings, commercial real estate, warehouses) → trust → investors own units → receive distributions.

Axis Capital describes itself as a pioneer in the Indian InvIT/REIT space. Source: Axis Capital

What does an IB analyst actually do?

Financial analysis

P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, EBITDA, Working Capital, Debt.

Valuation

DCF, trading comparables, transaction comparables, precedent transactions.

Deal execution

Pitch decks, information memorandums, management presentations, investor presentations, transaction documents.

Research & modelling

Industry research, competitor benchmarking, market sizing, investor research; building financial models to answer: what is the company worth, how much capital does it need, what happens under different scenarios?

Part 1 · Company

Institutional Equities

The basic difference from IB: Investment Banking helps the company raise money / execute a transaction. Institutional Equities helps investors understand, access and trade the market.

Must know

Axis Capital says its institutional-equities franchise serves approximately 120 domestic institutions and 232 foreign institutional investors. Source: Axis Capital Clients include mutual funds, insurance companies, FIIs, pension funds, sovereign funds and alternative investment funds.

Equity Research

The analytical side of Institutional Equities. The fundamental question: “What is this company worth, and is the stock attractive at the current price?”

Company

Revenue, costs, margins, cash flows, debt, capex, management, competitive advantage.

Industry

Market size, growth, competition, regulation, industry cycles, market share.

Valuation

P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B, DCF, sum-of-the-parts.

Output

An investment recommendation: BUY / HOLD / SELL.

Axis Capital’s institutional-equities platform currently says it covers approximately 288 stocks, representing around 69% of India’s market capitalisation, based on its FY26 Q3 disclosure. Source: AxisBank

Research isn’t only financial models — it involves company meetings, management interactions, industry research, channel checks, competitor analysis, macro research and thematic research.

Channel checks, illustrated

Suppose Asian Paints says demand is recovering. An analyst might speak to dealers, distributors, contractors and suppliers to test whether what management says is actually happening on the ground — this is why research is different from simply reading annual reports.

Sales & Trading

Where Institutional Equities meets the market. A large institutional investor may say “I want to buy ₹100 crore of Reliance” — the trading desk helps execute that order. But execution isn’t simply buy → done: the trader must consider liquidity, market impact, timing, order size, volatility, price, available counterparties and confidentiality.

Axis Capital offers both high-touch and low-touch execution across cash and derivatives. Source: Axis Capital

High-TouchLow-Touch
Human-intensive execution: client interacts with sales, trader, execution deskTechnology-driven execution: algorithms, electronic trading, data analytics, automated execution
Useful for large orders, complex trades, illiquid securities, situations requiring market intelligenceObjective: execute efficiently while managing market impact and transaction costs

What does a trading analyst do?

Trading requires a different mindset — understanding equity markets, market microstructure, liquidity, order types, volatility, derivatives, execution and risk. The trader constantly asks: “How do I execute this order without unnecessarily moving the market?”

Corporate Access

An interesting bridge between Research and Sales. Institutional investors want access to company management. Axis Capital can facilitate management meetings, investor conferences, roadshows, site visits, speaker sessions and analyst interactions.

Example: A mutual fund wants to understand a company’s capex plans → Axis Capital’s research/sales platform arranges a meeting with management.

IPO market becoming more selective

What happened

Axis Capital MD & CEO Atul Mehra said that only around 40% of the IPO pipeline might actually reach the market, because valuation expectations between issuers and investors had become a challenge. Source: Moneycontrol

His broader argument: the problem isn’t necessarily lack of capital, it’s valuation discipline. High-quality companies at reasonable valuations can attract demand; aggressively priced IPOs may struggle.

The “Axis Capital in 30 Seconds” Answer

If someone asks: “What exactly does Axis Capital do?”

“Axis Capital is the wholly owned investment banking and institutional equities subsidiary of Axis Bank, with roots in ENAM Securities. Its business has two broad pillars: Investment Banking and Institutional Equities. Investment Banking helps corporates, promoters, governments and financial sponsors with transactions such as IPOs, QIPs, OFS, M&A, private fundraising and InvITs and REITs. Institutional Equities serves investors through equity research, cash and derivatives execution, sales and corporate access. So, in simple terms, Axis Capital helps companies raise capital and execute strategic transactions while helping institutional investors research, access and trade Indian markets.”

The “Interviewer Can Go Anywhere” Rule

One of the biggest mistakes candidates make: they prepare questions in isolation. The actual interview may move like this:

TMAY → Why Finance? → Why IB? → Previous job → Industry → Company valuation → Stock pitch → Recent news → Axis deal → DCF → Why Axis? → Pressure → Role preference

You need to be able to move across domains without losing your structure.

Finance Technicals

Every candidate should know accounting basics: P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, PAT, Working Capital, Depreciation, Amortisation, Capex, Debt.

The Three Financial Statements

Income Statement

Measures profitability over a period.

Revenue − Expenses = EBITDA − Depreciation = EBIT − Interest − Taxes = PAT

Balance Sheet

Measures financial position at a point in time.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Cash Flow Statement

Explains how cash moved during the period, in three sections:

1. CFO   2. CFI   3. CFF

Three-statement question

Common question

“If depreciation increases by ₹10, what happens?” (assume tax = 25%)

P&L

EBIT ↓ ₹10 · Tax ↓ ₹2.5 · PAT ↓ ₹7.5

Cash Flow

PAT ↓ ₹7.5, Depreciation added back +₹10 → CFO ↑ ₹2.5

Balance Sheet

PP&E ↓ ₹10, Cash ↑ ₹2.5, Retained earnings ↓ ₹7.5 → Total assets ↓ ₹7.5 = Equity ↓ ₹7.5. Balanced.

This is the kind of question candidates should be able to answer without hesitation.

Working Capital

Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities (operationally: AR + Inventory − AP)

ChangeUsual effect
Inventory increasesCash gets tied up → CFO decreases
Accounts receivable increasesSales booked but cash not collected → CFO decreases
Accounts payable increasesCompany has delayed cash payment → CFO increases

EBITDA

EBITDA = Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortisation. Used as a rough measure of operating performance before financing, taxes and certain non-cash charges.

Watch out

EBITDA ≠ cash flow, because companies still have taxes, working-capital requirements, capex, interest and other cash expenses.

Valuation — Level 1

Equity Value

Value attributable to shareholders.

Enterprise Value

Approximate value attributable to all capital providers.

A common bridge: EV = Equity Value + Debt + Preferred Stock + Minority Interest − Cash. Know the logic, not just the formula.

P/E

P/E = Market Price per Share / EPS (or Equity Value / Net Income). Useful when comparing companies with reasonably comparable capital structures, accounting and business models.

EV/EBITDA

EV / EBITDA is useful because EV captures debt + equity while EBITDA is before interest — allowing more comparable operating-business valuation across different capital structures.

DCF

You should be able to explain DCF in 30 seconds: forecast future free cash flows → discount them to present value → calculate terminal value → add them → arrive at enterprise value → bridge to equity value.

WACC

Weighted Average Cost of Capital — the blended required return of the company’s capital providers. Conceptually: Cost of Equity × Equity Weight + After-tax Cost of Debt × Debt Weight.

Valuation decreases — future cash flows are discounted more heavily.

?What happens if terminal growth increases?

Valuation increases, because terminal cash flows become more valuable.

?Why can’t you use WACC for equity value directly?

Because WACC discounts FCFF, which produces Enterprise Value. For FCFE, you’d use cost of equity and arrive directly at equity value.

The three-layer current affairs framework

For every news story, don’t stop at “what happened.” Ask: Layer 1 — what happened? Layer 2 — why did it happen? Layer 3 — what does it mean?

Worked example: “What happens if RBI cuts rates?”

Lower policy rate → lower short-term borrowing costs → potentially higher credit demand → potential increase in consumption/investment → potentially stronger earnings. Nuance: impact differs by sector, funding structure and monetary transmission.

Trading — Detailed Preparation

Trading candidates should think differently from IB/ER candidates.

Market Structure

Know: bid, ask, spread, order book, liquidity, volume, volatility, market depth.

Worked example

Bid: ₹100 · Ask: ₹100.20 · Spread: ₹0.20

Market order vs limit order

Market orderLimit order
Execute immediately at available market prices. Advantage: execution certainty. Risk: price uncertainty.Execute only at a specified price or better. Advantage: price control. Risk: execution may not happen.

Market impact

Worked example

Normal daily volume = ₹10 crore. Client wants to sell ₹50 crore. Dumping the entire order immediately can push the price down. Execution might instead involve breaking the order, algorithms, timing, multiple venues and liquidity analysis — this is where trading becomes interesting.

Futures

A futures contract is an agreement to buy/sell an underlying asset at a future date at a predetermined price. Know: margin, mark-to-market, leverage, hedging, basis.

The most important preparation insight

There is a subtle but important difference between passing a finance test and passing an Axis interview. A finance test asks “Do you know the formula?” An Axis interview can ask: “You said you like Company X. Why?” → “Why is its margin increasing?” → “What if margins don’t increase?” → “How does that affect your DCF?” → “Would you still buy?” → “Okay. Tell me why you want to work here.” So the interview is testing depth + reasoning + communication, not just memory — consistent with Axis’s own emphasis on financial proficiency, analytical thinking, experience, actions and results. Source: Axis Capital
